## Setting

A small volunteer-run tool library and repair room in Milwaukee, Wisconsin, USA: **Riverwest Tool Share**, on a commercial side street near neighborhood homes and small storefronts. It lends basic household tools and hosts a monthly Saturday fix-it clinic. The room is practical rather than picturesque: pegboard, labeled bins, a scarred workbench, folding chairs, a whiteboard with volunteer shifts, and the faint smell of machine oil and coffee.

It is a weekday afternoon. Volunteers are preparing the room for Saturday's clinic. No emergency, supernatural event, hidden mystery, or countdown is present.

## Player position

The player is a new volunteer helping with clinic preparation. The player has no special authority over the organization and may propose, help, decline, or leave. The player has not met these NPCs before.

## NPCs

### Renee Carter — coordinator, 46
- Practical, careful, direct; not cold. She dislikes vague assurances and prefers a process that can be repeated.
- Coordinates the tool library and is responsible for loan policy.
- Current view: power tools are not lent out for unsupervised use by new members. Supervised demonstrations are allowed.
- GM-only personal fact, not yet player-known: when Renee was a teenager, her father ran a small repair shop. A customer borrowed a circular saw, returned it with a damaged guard, and later Renee's father had to explain the incident to an injured customer. Renee remembers the stress and the shop's responsibility more than the technical details. This shaped her preference for accountable handoffs. She does not regard all borrowers as careless.
- She may disclose a concise version if the conversation naturally reaches the reason for the policy. Do not volunteer a melodramatic monologue.

### Malik Turner — volunteer repair lead, 34
- Sociable, hands-on, impatient with bureaucracy but attentive to actual risk.
- Believes people learn safe tool use through guided practice. He prefers demonstration and direct observation over blanket prohibitions.
- He does not know Renee's private family story unless she tells him in the scene.
- He has seen first-time volunteers become competent after a supervised demonstration; this is his own experience, not proof that every borrower is safe.

### Tessa Nguyen — volunteer, 25
- Energetic, organized, quick to offer practical systems. Sometimes assumes a checklist solves more than it does.
- She wants Saturday's clinic to feel welcoming and is drafting a simple intake/sign-out sheet.
- She has not heard Renee's personal story and should not refer to it unless it is shared in her presence.
- She is not a mere chorus: she can make proposals and take initiative within her role.
