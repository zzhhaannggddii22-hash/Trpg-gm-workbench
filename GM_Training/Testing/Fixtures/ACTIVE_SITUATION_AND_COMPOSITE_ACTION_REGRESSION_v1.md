# Active Situation & Composite Action Regression v1

> Status: TEST FIXTURE
> Canon: TEST_ONLY_NON_CANON
> Scope: Generic GM runtime, with a Cairn 2e / EBB-style travel encounter as the example.
> This fixture does not alter any Campaign, Session, character sheet, or live table state.

## 1. Purpose

Test whether the GM can host one evolving encounter and its aftermath as part of a continuous journey, while reducing unnecessary interaction turns without compressing away meaningful process.

This fixture specifically targets the failure mode where:
- a player says "run" and the GM immediately declares the whole party escaped;
- the GM turns every stage into a separate Save or a separate user reply;
- the GM rolls another random encounter immediately after the first encounter, without reaching the module's next trigger boundary;
- the GM compresses injury, companion behavior, and travel interludes into a status report.

## 2. Fixed illustrative setup

The following is a training setup, not Campaign CANON:

- A night-traveling group has triggered a warning trap.
- Two companions have been injured. One companion is physically struggling to keep pace and is frightened, but tends to conceal fear with self-talk or brisk remarks.
- The player character is mobile, has a ranged weapon, and can choose to run, cover the group, split the formation, wait, hide, or try another plausible approach.
- Hostile creatures have been alerted nearby. Their exact position, numbers, visibility, intentions, and willingness to pursue must come only from the module, the actual dice, and the established scene; do not invent information for dramatic convenience.
- The event has already been rolled for the current prescribed time interval.

Any sample line such as "You're fine... you got out last time, didn't you?" is illustrative only, not a prescribed quote or persistent character fact.

## 3. Test input sequence

1. Player: "Damn it, run! Don't stay here!"
2. Player: "Help the slower companion; perhaps send the escort ahead while I stay in the middle and use my sling to discourage anything that comes close."
3. Player: After the immediate pressure eases, "Let's bandage the wounds, take a breather, then continue."
4. Player: "Continue onward."

The fixture does not prescribe the outcome of these actions. The GM must adjudicate from the actual scene, rules, dice, opponents, and time.

## 4. Required behavior

### A. Preserve an active situation

- Treat "run" as an escape plan, not a completed escape.
- Keep the current threat active until the fiction and rules establish that it has been avoided, escaped, defeated, negotiated with, or otherwise ceased to matter.
- Show relevant movement constraints, distance, terrain, light, injury, and companion behavior when they affect choices.
- Do not invent extra obstacles or enemy actions simply to stretch the encounter.
- Do not force a chase sequence if the opponents' goals, capabilities, or morale do not support one.

### B. Preserve player tactics and agency

- Evaluate the player's combined plan as a whole; do not split it into unnecessary micro-turns.
- Resolve clearly implied, low-risk actions together.
- Stop at an actual tactical fork: for example, whether the player stays to cover the companion, takes a shot, abandons cover, or changes direction.
- Do not choose the player's attack target, promise, risk acceptance, or next tactic.
- An ally's difficulty does not automatically mean failure or teleportation. Present the real situation and let player actions matter.

### C. Use rules accurately and efficiently

- Apply the correct rule to each real risk. Do not create a Save for ordinary movement without a relevant risk.
- Batch independent rolls arising from one trigger (e.g. all affected characters' trap Saves and damage); keep separate target-specific results and state deltas.
- Do not batch dependent outcomes before their trigger exists. An enemy's later reaction to the player's covering action is adjudicated after that action.
- If the table convention assigns dice to the GM, roll for PCs and NPCs consistently; do not ask the player to roll unless the player has explicitly chosen to do so.
- A Save resolves only its specific risk. It does not automatically resolve the entire escape plan or encounter.

### D. Respect the module's time procedure

- The current interval's encounter check has already happened.
- Do not roll a second encounter merely because the player takes another action, pauses briefly, treats wounds, or resumes the same leg of travel.
- Trigger the next encounter check only at the next prescribed module time boundary or explicit trigger.
- Track real costs that matter to the deadline; do not round every small action to a full hour without rule or module support.

### E. Preserve aftermath and ordinary journey

- After the immediate threat ends, let the party deal with injuries, regroup, recover light, re-order equipment, talk, rest, or continue in silence as the fiction and player choice support.
- Preserve sensory and character detail where it contributes to the shared journey; do not mistake shorter output for better pacing.
- Do not force every companion to speak. Their attention and response should reflect their established character and current condition.
- Do not immediately replace the resolved encounter with another encounter-table result unless the next trigger has actually arrived.

## 5. Fast self-check rubric

Mark PASS / PARTIAL / FAIL with direct evidence from the response:

1. **Situation:** Was the encounter kept active until its actual resolution?
2. **Agency:** Did the player's plan remain a plan until its consequences were established?
3. **Batching:** Were independent same-trigger mechanics combined without skipping conditional follow-up?
4. **Clock:** Was the next random event triggered only at the correct boundary?
5. **Journey:** Did the aftermath/interlude remain part of the same journey without forced filler or abrupt scene reset?
6. **Tempo:** Did one response cover the obvious action chain and stop at a meaningful decision, rather than asking for one micro-action at a time?

A self-authored sample or static review is only SYNTHETIC_SELF_REVIEW. It does not establish REAL_LIVE_SESSION success. Record unresolved issues instead of declaring a pass from the existence of this fixture.

## 6. Non-goals

- No new chase subsystem, extra clocks, NPC schedules, per-round companion task list, or encounter state machine.
- No requirement to narrate every footstep or every sentence of dialogue.
- No changes to Cairn rules, EBB encounter frequency, danger, resource costs, or player control.
- No new hidden facts, module clues, or forced narrative links.
