## 2. Magnitude

### BLD-ARC-01｜Magnitude is a judgment scale, not a universal formula

Magnitude estimates the quality/power of a supernatural entity or force and can describe different dimensions: area, scale, duration, range, force, and quality. It is a comparison aid. Do not mechanically add every dimension, and do not treat a listed level as a hard cap.

| Magnitude | Area | Scale | Duration | Range | Quality | Force |
|---:|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Closet | 1–2 people | Moments | Within reach | Poor | Weak |
| 1 | Small room | Small gang (3–6) | Minutes | A dozen paces | Adequate | Moderate |
| 2 | Large room | Medium gang (~12) | Hour | Stone's throw | Good | Strong |
| 3 | Several rooms | Large gang (~20) | Few hours | Down the road | Excellent | Serious |
| 4 | Small building | Huge gang (~40) | Day | Several blocks | Superior | Powerful |
| 5 | Large building | Massive gang (~80) | Several days | Across district | Impeccable | Overwhelming |
| 6 | City block | Colossal gang (~160) | Week | Across city | Legendary | Devastating |

Quality examples help calibrate mundane/arcane asset quality: 0 rusty knife or rickety shack; 1 fighting blade/ordinary clothing; 2 pistol/private room/typical ghost; 3 coach/boat/military rifle; 4 luxury vehicle/typical demon or powerful ghost; 5 small ship/lightning barrier; 6 large ship/rare essence/arcane artifact/powerful demon.

**GM procedure**
1. State what the force/entity/item is trying to accomplish.
2. Identify the dimension that actually constrains the result (force, range, area, duration, scale, quality).
3. Compare it to the nearest examples; use one dimension where sufficient.
4. Combine dimensions only when the fiction demands genuinely combined magnitude. Record why each level is included.
5. Use Magnitude as a Fortune Roll dice pool only when the degree of manifestation is uncertain and Magnitude is the relevant trait.
6. For crafting/acquisition, use Magnitude as a guideline for minimum Quality; it is not an automatic one-to-one formula.

Do not add area to force by default. An entity can generate a powerful effect whose area is simply part of what that effect naturally does. Conversely, a district-wide effect may require combining force and range when both are extraordinary.

## 3. Rituals

### BLD-ARC-02｜Find a source and learn a ritual

**Eligibility:** the PC needs the Ritual Special Ability to safely study/practice ritual sorcery. A PC with Ritual begins with one known ritual, defined with the GM. A PC without Ritual should not casually research or perform rituals; the official rule warns that such study leaves the character vulnerable to the powers being supplicated.

**Source first:** obtain a source through Score Payoff, theft, a contact, cultist access, ancient text, or a Long-Term Project. If the PC does not yet have access to a source, establishing that access is a separate fictional obstacle/project.

**Learning:** after securing a source, begin a Long-Term Project; most rituals use an 8-segment clock. Resolve project actions normally. During definition, record:

1. Effect and weirdness: what does the ritual do, and what uncanny detail marks it?
2. Method and price: what must the caster do, what materials/target/site/time are required, and what does it cost?
3. Occult consequence: what new belief or fear does knowing this ritual instill?

These answers form the ritual's reusable specification. If a new source, new purpose, or changed fictional circumstances materially changes the ritual, reopen the questions rather than assuming a universal spell effect.

### BLD-ARC-03｜Perform a known ritual

**Preconditions**
- The caster has the Ritual ability and knows this ritual.
- The recorded method, target, materials, location, and any prerequisites are satisfied.
- The ritual's time and Downtime Activity cost are clear.

**Execution**
1. Confirm the ritual's intended effect and the current circumstances. Ritual performance is a unique event; do not assume it is a perfectly repeatable spell button.
2. Spend at least one Downtime Activity. Very powerful or far-reaching rituals may require two or more.
3. Apply the previously established Stress cost, using Magnitude as the guideline. A ritual's actual cost is set during its definition; do not recompute it mechanically from a single magnitude number every time.
4. Longer casting time can reduce Stress cost. Use the Magnitude duration examples as a guide; the official guidance generally does not reduce casting below about an hour. Longer casting can create exposure, delay, or a chance for targets to react.
5. Apply any additional price already defined: rare materials, sacrifice, a target's participation, a site, or a dire Clock.
6. If performance is dangerous/troublesome, make an Action Roll (usually Attune) to resolve the danger and its consequences.
7. If the effect's degree or success is uncertain, make a Fortune Roll. For a Downtime Fortune Roll, Coin may be spent 1-for-1 to raise the result level after the roll.
8. If it is both dangerous and uncertain, both rolls may be required: Action Roll for the danger of performing it; Fortune Roll for how well the intended effect manifests.
9. Consider ticking a Clock that advances an arcane entity's agenda or the dark consequence of using the ritual. Only create/advance it when supported by the established ritual and fiction.
10. Record Stress, Coin/material expenditure, consumed resources, clocks, target/effect, duration, and any primed state.

**Primed rituals:** a ritual may be partly performed during Downtime and released later by a final incantation/action. Record `primed: true`, the exact completion trigger, remaining time/cost, and what can interrupt it. Do not treat priming as a free instant cast.

**Repeat use:** each performance can be a unique event. If the context, scale, target, or intended effect changes, revisit the ritual questions and costs. If a ritual has become too broad or safe through play, re-establish its weirdness and costs with the player.

## 4. Invention

### BLD-ARC-04｜Invent a formula or design

Inventing is a Long-Term Project, not the same action as crafting the finished item. Most new formulas/designs require an 8-segment clock. Use Study for the project unless the fictional method calls for another fitting action.

**Creation definition**
1. Type and function: mundane, alchemical, arcane, or spark-craft; what does it do in play?
2. Minimum Quality: GM sets the minimum using the Magnitude of its effects as a guideline.
3. Obscurity: player explains the rare, strange, adverse, or inaccessible feature that kept it out of common use.
4. Drawbacks: GM chooses suitable drawbacks, or explicitly says there are none.

Record the design as a specification, not a free-form wish: intended effect, range/area/duration, minimum Quality, required materials/tools, production time, uses, drawbacks, and any special trigger.

**Known design exceptions**
- Alchemist begins with one special alchemical formula already known.
- Artificer begins with one special spark-craft design already known.
- Strange Methods begins with one special arcane design already known.
- These abilities also provide +1 result level when inventing or crafting the matching creation type.

Once invented, only the inventor can craft that special design unless another character learns it through their own Long-Term Project. Common alchemicals and ordinary items using commonly available instructions do not require a special invention project.

## 5. Crafting

### BLD-ARC-05｜Craft a known creation

Crafting takes one Downtime Activity and a Tinker roll. The dice pool is 1d per Tinker Action dot. Determine the base Quality from Crew Tier:

| Tinker result | Produced Quality |
|---|---:|
| 1–3 | Crew Tier -1 |
| 4/5 | Crew Tier |
| 6 | Crew Tier +1 |
| Critical | Crew Tier +2 |

Then apply:
- Workshop: effective Tier is +1 for this crafting roll.
- Spend Coin: +1 final Quality per Coin spent; this can exceed Tier+2.
- Alchemist: +1 result level when the creation has alchemical features.
- Artificer: +1 result level when the creation has spark-craft features.
- Strange Methods: +1 result level when the creation has arcane features.

Set the required minimum Quality before the roll. Magnitude guides the requirement, but GM may adjust to suit the project. If the final result is below minimum, do not silently produce the full intended effect; establish what lesser/failed result the fiction supports and whether further work is needed.

### BLD-ARC-06｜Modify an existing item

Modification does not require inventing a new formula/design. Use a crafting roll with the item's baseline Quality equal to Crew Tier:

| Modification scope | Required Quality |
|---|---:|
| Simple, useful feature | Tier +1 |
| Significant modification | Tier +2 |
| Arcane, spark-craft, or alchemical modification | Tier +3 |

Apply the same relevant crafting bonuses, Coin, Workshop, and drawbacks. Do not classify a fundamentally new item or independent effect as a trivial modification merely to bypass invention.

## 6. Drawbacks

### BLD-ARC-07｜Creation constraints

GM selects one or more drawbacks where appropriate and records them in the item's persistent specification:

| Drawback | Runtime consequence |
|---|---|
| Complex | Multi-stage production; each stage costs one Downtime Activity and one crafting roll. GM sets stage count. |
| Conspicuous | If used any number of times during an operation, crew takes +1 Heat for that operation. |
| Consumable | Limited uses; all alchemicals must be consumable, usually one use. |
| Rare | Requires a rare material/item during crafting. |
| Unreliable | On each use, Fortune Roll using the item's Quality to determine performance. |
| Volatile | Dangerous/troublesome side-effect defined by GM; it is a consequence and may be resisted. |

Do not add drawbacks as arbitrary punishment after the player has committed resources. Define them during invention/modification, or explain the new fictional development that causes a change.

## 7. Alchemical and crafted item runtime

### BLD-ARC-08｜Use and state tracking

- A known design is permission to craft it, not a free item. Track each crafted batch/item, Quality, remaining uses, required materials, and active drawbacks.
- Alchemicals are consumable; a crafted alchemical normally has one use unless the established specification says otherwise.
- If an item is Unreliable, roll Fortune using its Quality each time it is used; do not substitute an Action Roll unless the use itself is dangerous/troublesome and an action roll is warranted.
- If an item is Volatile, resolve its side effect as a consequence; the user may resist where the normal rules permit.
- If a crafted item is Conspicuous, apply +1 Heat once per Score/operation in which it is used, not once per individual use in that operation.
- Acquiring restricted alchemicals/poisons/bombs/dangerous gadgets rather than crafting them may carry the separate +2 Heat rule; do not confuse this with the Conspicuous drawback.
- A PC's Alchemist/Artificer/Strange Methods ability, a Crew Claim, Workshop, and a crafted item's own Quality are separate modifiers/state fields. Apply only those whose trigger is met.

## 8. Fast decision tree

```text
Need an occult effect?
  ├─ Existing known ritual → Ritual performance procedure
  ├─ New ritual → secure source → 8-clock learning project → define ritual
  └─ Not a ritual, but a designed item?
       ├─ New special design → invention project → define minimum Quality/drawbacks
       ├─ Known/common design → craft with Tinker during Downtime
       └─ Existing item + new function → modification Quality threshold
```
