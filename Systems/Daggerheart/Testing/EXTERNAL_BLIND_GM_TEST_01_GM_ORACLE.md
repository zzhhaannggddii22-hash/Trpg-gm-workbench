# External Blind GM Test 01 — GM Oracle

**GM ONLY — DO NOT SHOW TO PLAYER**

## Rules and data binding

- Ruleset: Daggerheart Core Set, Tier 1.
- Primary Environment: Raging River, Tier 1 Traversal, Difficulty 10.
- Use the exact local Core Set records in:
  - Reference_Data/Core_Set/environments.json — Raging River
  - Reference_Data/Core_Set/adversaries.json — Glass Snake, Jagged Knife Bandit
- This test checks GM execution, not solo encounter balance. Do not infer encounter balance from the scene.

## Hidden truth

Tamsin is alive, stranded on a broad rock in the middle of the river. Her waterproof satchel is tied to the rock with a short cord. She can signal with a small mirror when the party looks toward the water.

The punt's guide rope was cut cleanly with a knife. A Jagged Knife Bandit scout cut it to isolate the courier and create an opportunity to steal the dispatch. The scout is watching from the far-bank bluff and wants the satchel, not a fight to the death.

A Glass Snake is hunting in the river. It is not responsible for the cut rope and is not allied with the bandits.

Halen, the ferryman, noticed the cut rope but did not see who did it. He fears losing his livelihood and is reluctant to risk the punt. He is practical rather than deceptive. If treated respectfully or offered a credible safety plan, he will share what he knows and help.

Jory saw a person with a knife near the rope before dawn. He did not see the person's face. He also saw a flash from the middle of the river but does not know it was Tamsin's mirror.

## NPC motives and behavior

### Halen — ferryman
- Motive: protect his boat, livelihood, and nephew; avoid another drowning.
- Voice: concise, practical, cautious. He asks about ropes, weight, and the current rather than giving lore speeches.
- Knows: the rope was cut cleanly; the current is stronger near the north bank; Tamsin left before dawn.
- Does not know: the Glass Snake's location or the bandit's identity.
- Reaction: helps when the PCs offer a workable plan; refuses reckless demands but does not become hostile without cause.

### Jory — child lookout
- Motive: be useful and be taken seriously.
- Voice: short observations, quick corrections, occasional exaggeration.
- Knows: saw a knife-bearing silhouette near the rope; saw a mirror flash midstream.
- Does not know: why the rope was cut or what is in the satchel.

### Tamsin — courier
- Motive: survive and deliver the dispatch.
- Position: stranded on the midstream rock.
- Can signal when the party looks toward the river or creates a clear opening.
- Is injured but alert; do not make her helpless or unconscious merely to force a rescue scene.

### Bandit scout
- Motive: steal the dispatch and escape.
- Position: concealed on the far-bank bluff.
- Prefers to exploit distraction, not fight to the death.
- Only becomes an active adversary if the party exposes the satchel, approaches the bluff, or gives the scout a clear opportunity.

## Environment procedure: Raging River

Use the local Raging River stat block as written.

- Crossing the river requires the party to complete a Progress Countdown (4).
- Advance it on meaningful progress toward crossing, not for every line of dialogue or every trivial inspection.
- Players can choose different approaches: rope, punt, swimming, magic, teamwork, or another credible plan. Respect the fiction; do not force a single solution.
- If a PC rolls Failure with Fear while attempting the crossing, they are immediately targeted by the Undertow action without requiring the GM to spend Fear on that feature. Do not charge a second Fear for this automatic trigger.
- The Undertow action can also be activated independently by spending 1 Fear. The target makes an Agility Reaction Roll. On a failure, apply the local feature: 1d6+1 physical damage, move them Close downriver, and make them Vulnerable until they get out of the river. On a success, they mark Stress.
- Patient Hunter is a Fear Feature: spend 1 Fear to summon a Glass Snake within Close range of a chosen PC; the Snake immediately takes the Spotlight to use Spinning Serpent. The Fear Feature's cost is paid once. Do not add a generic extra-Spotlight cost on top of the feature's explicitly granted Spotlight.
- The Glass Snake's Spinning Serpent costs the Snake 1 Stress and makes an attack against all targets within Very Close range.
- The Glass Snake's Spitter wording about taking the Spotlight a second time this GM Turn is a disputed edge case (DH-I-020). Avoid using Spitter in this test unless the evaluator explicitly wants to probe that unresolved rule.

## Clue map

Clues are discoverable through reasonable questions and actions. Do not require a roll for trivial observations.

| Clue | What it establishes |
|---|---|
| The guide rope has a clean knife cut, not frayed fibers | Someone deliberately cut it |
| Jory saw a knife-bearing silhouette before dawn | A person was near the rope |
| A mirror flashes from the midstream rock | Tamsin is alive and stranded |
| The current is visibly stronger near the north bank | A safer crossing line may exist |
| The scout's footprints lead from the rope toward the bluff | The scout is watching the river |
| A glassy ripple moves against the current | A Glass Snake is nearby; this is not the source of the cut rope |

Do not reveal the full explanation when a single clue is found. Let the player connect evidence.

## Pacing and GM guidance

- Start with the river, Halen's immediate concern, and Jory's observation. Do not deliver the hidden truth as an exposition block.
- Ask what the PCs do. Do not present a long list of prescribed options.
- Do not call for an Action Roll unless the action is uncertain and failure would matter.
- Let straightforward questions and low-risk observations succeed automatically.
- Keep NPCs distinct through motives, priorities, and speech rhythm.
- The bandit scout is an optional threat. Do not force combat.
- The Glass Snake is a separate environmental threat; do not imply that it cut the rope.
- Respect successful plans. A clever alternative should change the procedure, not be punished for avoiding the expected route.
- Keep the scene moving. Do not simulate every minute or repeat already established facts.

## Test end condition

The scene ends when the party rescues Tamsin and secures the dispatch, abandons the attempt, or reaches a clear new situation. The dispatch's delivery is a natural closing beat, not a forced epilogue.
